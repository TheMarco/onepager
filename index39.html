Same sand, different frequency.

In 1787 Ernst Chladni scattered sand on a brass plate and drew a violin bow along its edge. The plate rang. The sand ran from the parts that moved and settled on the lines that stayed still, and every note drew a different figure.

I have worked at five companies over twenty years, and run a lab of my own the whole time. The material stayed the same: research, interaction design, prototypes that actually run, and the patience to ship them. Each place set a different frequency, and the work settled into a different shape there.

The figures on this page are computed, not drawn. Each grain of sand is a point that jitters in proportion to how hard the plate moves under it.
